Sealing Drafts in Wall Cavities

Air leakage inside wall cavities can quietly undermine comfort, energy performance, and overall building durability. Careful air sealing at framing joints, penetrations, and transitions helps reduce drafts before they move through the assembly, and closed cell spray foam insulation can be part of that strategy when a project calls for added resistance in tight spaces. A well planned approach also considers how materials interact with the rest of the wall, while closed cell spray foam insulation supports more consistent coverage around irregular areas.

To improve results, contractors often focus on both installation quality and the sequence of work. Gaps around wiring, plumbing, and rim areas can create hidden pathways for unwanted movement, so detailing must remain precise from one section to the next. In that context, closed cell spray foam insulation is often evaluated alongside other air control measures, and closed cell spray foam insulation can help reinforce continuity where framing leaves small voids or hard to reach corners.

When wall cavities are sealed with a complete system in mind, the finished enclosure is more likely to perform reliably over time.
